# Living Cost Comparison — competitor analysis and product decision document

**Status:** Accepted product direction  
**Research date:** 21 September 2026  
**Domain:** `livingcostcomparison.com`  
**Scope:** Product positioning, competitive requirements, information architecture, page content, data strategy and organic-search growth.  
**Related:** [Website foundation](./website-plan.md) and [brand kit](./brand-kit.md).

## Executive decision

Living Cost Comparison will not try to beat established competitors by publishing the largest possible directory of generic city pages. Numbeo and Livingcost.org already own that breadth, while Mercer and ECA own the high-priced corporate research end of the market.

We will build the most useful consumer decision path for a move, remote-work base or salary offer:

> **Compare what life will cost, what salary will preserve it, and how reliable the estimate is.**

The product will combine:

1. A fast city-to-city answer.
2. A household- and lifestyle-adjusted monthly budget.
3. Gross and take-home salary equivalence, with tax kept separate from living costs.
4. Category and item-level evidence behind the answer.
5. Visible source, coverage, update date and confidence information.

This positioning deliberately sits between two common competitor extremes: large but impersonal price databases, and simple salary calculators that hide most assumptions.

## What this research can and cannot say

The competitors below were selected because they repeatedly appear for relevant search intents, provide comparable products, or set a quality benchmark for cost-of-living data. This is a product/SERP review, not an estimate of traffic or market share. Traffic, backlink and keyword-volume claims require a separate Ahrefs, Semrush or Search Console dataset.

## Detailed competitor findings and product response

### Numbeo

**Product pattern.** Numbeo offers city and country comparisons, current and historical indexes, item-level prices, property information, quality-of-life categories and several specialized salary and relocation products. Its API can return indices, individual prices, currency conversion, contributor counts and recency fields.

**SEO pattern.** A very large, interconnected directory connects city pages, country pages, rankings, historical pages and adjacent calculators.

**Trust pattern.** Crowdsourcing enables scale. Its premium quality reports expose contributor volume, price dispersion, percentiles, data age and confidence measures.

**Decision for us.** We need item-level drill-down, contributor/sample or observation counts, freshness and uncertainty. We should not copy the dense navigation or expose every possible dataset on the first screen.

### Expatistan

**Product pattern.** Expatistan centers city comparison and salary conversion around an expat-oriented basket. Its published sample salary reports describe dozens of products and services across six broad categories.

**Trust pattern.** The service describes continuous online collection, statistical validation, removal of spurious data and external cross-checking. Its reports disclose how many prices and survey responses support the calculation.

**Decision for us.** Every result needs a visible evidence summary: number of usable observations, most recent period, share directly observed and important gaps. A single unexplained confidence badge is not sufficient.

### Livingcost.org

**Product pattern.** Livingcost.org combines a very large city/country directory with comparisons, rankings and a transparent basket. It distinguishes single-person and family costs and supplements crowdsourced observations with public datasets and estimation models.

**SEO pattern.** Country hubs and thousands of city entities create strong topic coverage and internal-link density.

**Decision for us.** We should copy the entity structure, not the launch scale. Pages may be generated only after passing a data-quality and uniqueness gate. Modeled values must be labeled as modeled, never presented as observed local prices.

### CityCostComparison

**Product pattern.** The site expands from comparisons into purchasing power, salary, childcare, healthcare, housing, retirement, remote-work and inflation tools. Results are free, require no account and can be shared through URLs.

**Decision for us.** Shareable state and a compact group of adjacent calculators are valuable. We should resist publishing many near-identical calculators until each has a distinct job, dataset and search intent.

### NerdWallet

**Product pattern.** Three inputs—current city, new city and pre-tax household income—produce an equivalent income and category differences. Item tables make the percentages tangible, and results link into broader city-life content.

**Trust pattern.** A named editorial process and licensed C2ER data support the product. The calculator states important limitations, including the exclusion of taxes and metro-level housing geography.

**Decision for us.** Lead with an answer, then show the evidence. Always state geographic level and tax treatment next to the result rather than burying these facts in methodology.

### SmartAsset

**Product pattern.** A guided flow asks for current and destination locations, pre-tax income, adults and children. The result considers taxes and large household expenses, then connects to broader financial tools.

**Decision for us.** Adults, children and tenure are high-value inputs. Taxes deserve a dedicated calculation layer. The result must distinguish "living-cost equivalent" from "take-home equivalent" so users can see the effect of each.

### Bankrate

**Product pattern.** The calculator sits inside a well-maintained editorial page with author, editor/reviewer, definitions, category education and sources.

**Decision for us.** Every important template needs ownership, review status, sources and a meaningful update date. We should keep the article secondary to the task instead of forcing users through generic copy before the calculator.

### Salary.com

**Product pattern.** Salary.com combines current salary, job title, home/work location, cost change and likely local pay change. It creates state, city and pair pages around the same salary ecosystem.

**Decision for us.** Cost-of-living equivalence and local market pay are different concepts and should appear side by side only when separate datasets support them. We must not imply that an employer will automatically pay the calculated equivalent.

### BestPlaces

**Product pattern.** BestPlaces combines living costs with taxes, housing, childcare, healthcare, crime and other place attributes. Its filters make it useful for people who have not chosen a destination.

**Decision for us.** An eventual "find an affordable city" experience is valuable, but it belongs after trustworthy comparison and city data. Quality-of-life attributes must not be blended into the cost index.

### Mercer

**Product pattern.** Mercer collects a large professional basket for global mobility, supports multiple shopper types and handles accommodation, education, business travel and allowance calculations.

**Decision for us.** User archetypes, housing choices and education costs are essential for international families. Our differentiation is accessibility and clarity; our methodology must accurately describe its more limited research scope.

## Product positioning

### Category

Consumer relocation and salary decision tool.

### Primary audience

1. A professional evaluating a job offer in another city or country.
2. A household planning an international or domestic move.
3. A remote worker choosing between possible bases.
4. A student or early-career mover building a realistic monthly budget.

Employers, researchers and relocation professionals are a later audience, not the initial interface driver.

### Core promise

**Compare the real cost of living for your household—not an abstract average.**

### Supporting proof

- A monthly budget users can inspect and change.
- Salary equivalence that states whether it is gross or take-home.
- Local and reference currency views.
- Sources, geography, date and confidence attached to each category.
- Clear separation of observed, calculated and modeled values.

### What the product is not

- Not a generic travel-cost calculator.
- Not a "best places" ranking disguised as objective truth.
- Not a salary survey until licensed job-market data is present.
- Not a mass-generated city directory with invented local prose.
- Not a promise that a calculated equivalent salary is what an employer will pay.

## Product decisions

### D1 — Decision tool before database

The main product journey starts with a move or salary decision, not a giant table. The index and directories support discovery, while the personalized comparison remains the primary conversion action.

### D2 — Take-home and living costs are separate layers

The base comparison measures spending. A tax module then calculates gross-to-net income by jurisdiction. Results show both:

- **Spending-equivalent salary:** preserves the selected basket.
- **Take-home-equivalent salary:** preserves disposable income after estimated taxes.

### D3 — Household and lifestyle materially change the answer

The default scenario will collect adults, children, housing tenure and a lifestyle preset. Users can then edit category weights or individual expenses.

### D4 — Minimum, balanced and comfortable are distinct baskets

The labels must be defined. "Minimum" means basic needs and is not the same as an average. "Balanced" represents a documented mainstream basket. "Comfortable" allows more housing space and discretionary spending. No label implies a moral judgment.

### D5 — One canonical URL per unordered city pair

`london-vs-amsterdam` and `amsterdam-vs-london` must not compete as duplicate pages. One normalized pair URL is canonical. A user can switch the move direction or salary origin inside that page without creating a second indexable URL.

### D6 — Programmatic SEO is quality-gated

A page becomes indexable only when it has adequate data coverage, original computed insight, useful item/category detail, a visible evidence block and meaningful related links. Thin or mostly modeled pages remain `noindex` or are not generated.

### D7 — Show local currency and a common comparison currency

Every price retains its native currency and date. The UI can show a selected reference currency, with exchange-rate source and timestamp. Currency conversion never masquerades as cost inflation.

### D8 — Confidence is explainable

A label such as high/medium/low is backed by visible components: source quality, observation count, recency, geographic match and dispersion. Users can open the evidence panel without leaving the result.

### D9 — Cost and quality of life remain separate

Safety, climate, schools, healthcare quality and commute time may help a relocation decision, but they do not enter the living-cost index. They can appear in a later "city fit" layer with their own sources.

### D10 — No open crowdsourcing at launch

User submissions can scale coverage, but only after identity/reputation controls, duplicate detection, outlier rules, review queues and a published contribution policy exist.

## Page-by-page content specification

### 1. Homepage — `/`

**Search/user job:** Understand the product and begin a comparison.

**Unique role:** Orientation and entry point, not a duplicate of the comparison hub.

**Content order:**

1. Header with Compare cities, Salary, City index, Methodology and one primary Compare action.
2. Hero: "Compare the real cost of living" plus two city inputs and optional household setup.
3. Immediate example result showing the type of answer—not a fake live claim.
4. Three outcomes: monthly budget, salary needed and what changes most.
5. Popular verified comparisons.
6. Small city-index preview with a link to the full explorer.
7. How the calculation works in three plain-language steps.
8. Trust strip: sources, last updated, observed/modeled policy and evidence-panel preview.
9. Audience paths: moving for work, moving with family, working remotely.
10. Editorial guides and methodology link.
11. Short FAQ based on real objections.

**Do not add:** a long generic definition before the tool, invented testimonials, decorative statistics or many unrelated cards.

### 2. Compare-cities hub — `/compare-cities/`

**Search/user job:** Choose two cities or discover popular comparisons.

**Unique role:** Comparison workbench and pair directory.

**Content order:**

1. Compact city-pair selector.
2. Household and lifestyle setup, collapsed after completion.
3. Popular and recently viewed pairs.
4. Browse by origin country and destination region.
5. Explanation of what is included/excluded.
6. Links to salary comparison and index for alternate tasks.

This page should not contain the full homepage education or a preselected result that makes navigation feel unchanged.

### 3. City-pair page — `/compare-cities/[a]-vs-[b]/`

**Search/user job:** Compare two named cities and decide whether a move or offer is financially viable.

**Content order:**

1. Direct answer: overall percentage, monthly difference and salary equivalent.
2. Scenario summary: household, housing, lifestyle, currencies and calculation date.
3. Category contribution chart showing what drives the gap.
4. Monthly budget table for both cities.
5. Item-level comparisons with units and local prices.
6. Housing section with rent/own and geographic definition.
7. Gross and take-home salary section.
8. "What this means" interpretation generated from verified differences, not generic filler.
9. Data quality/evidence panel and known gaps.
10. Visible calculation assumptions and methodology link.
11. Related city profiles and canonical comparisons.
12. Page-specific FAQ.

**Metadata pattern:** `London vs Amsterdam Cost of Living: Prices & Salary (2026)`  
**H1 pattern:** `Cost of living in London vs Amsterdam`

### 4. Salary calculator — `/salary-comparison/`

**Search/user job:** Evaluate an offer or calculate the salary needed after moving.

**Unique role:** Income-first workflow; it must not look like the generic city comparison.

**Content order:**

1. Guided input: current city, destination, gross/net salary, pay period, adults/children and housing.
2. Primary result: equivalent salary and minimum acceptable offer range.
3. Gross-to-net bridge showing taxes separately.
4. Disposable-income comparison after the selected budget.
5. Sensitivity view for rent and exchange-rate changes.
6. Explanation of equivalent salary versus local market salary.
7. Negotiation checklist and downloadable/shareable summary later.
8. Assumptions, evidence and FAQ.

### 5. City index — `/cost-of-living-index/`

**Search/user job:** Scan, sort and filter cities by affordability.

**Unique role:** Research table, not a calculator clone.

**Content order:**

1. Searchable table immediately visible.
2. Filters for region, household type, housing included/excluded and confidence.
3. Columns for overall, housing, groceries, transport, utilities, healthcare, local salary/purchasing power and freshness where available.
4. Base-city/base-index explanation fixed near the table.
5. Small methodology note and data-coverage summary.
6. Links from every row to the city entity; compare selection supports two rows.
7. Historical trend view only after repeated observations are trustworthy.

### 6. City profile — `/cost-of-living/[country]/[city]/`

**Search/user job:** Learn the realistic monthly cost of living in one named city.

**Content order:**

1. Snapshot with single, couple and family monthly budgets.
2. Housing range and exact geographic boundary.
3. Category totals and representative item prices.
4. Salary and purchasing-power context, clearly sourced.
5. Budget builder with lifestyle and housing controls.
6. What is unusually expensive or affordable, derived from the dataset.
7. Data quality, update date and source mix.
8. Popular comparisons from this city.
9. Country context and relevant guides.
10. City-specific FAQ grounded in available data.

City copy must not make neighborhood, tax, visa, school or safety claims without suitable local data.

### 7. Country hub — `/countries/[country]/`

**Search/user job:** Compare cities within a country and understand country-level cost context.

**Content order:** country snapshot, covered cities, cost range, currency/inflation note, tax-system link, highest/lowest covered cities, common internal comparisons, sources and freshness.

Country averages must not be presented as substitutes for city prices.

### 8. Rankings — `/rankings/[topic]/`

**Search/user job:** Discover places meeting one measurable affordability criterion.

Every ranking needs a precise metric, eligibility threshold, reference date, coverage count and reproducible methodology. Avoid vague composite titles such as "best cities" until a defensible quality-of-life model exists.

### 9. Guides — `/guides/[topic]/`

**Search/user job:** Understand a decision that the calculator alone cannot explain.

Guides should use original calculations, worked examples and links into live tools. They must not paraphrase competitor definitions. Initial topics:

- How to compare cost of living between two cities.
- Cost of living versus cost of labor.
- Gross versus net salary when moving countries.
- How rent changes a cost-of-living comparison.
- How to build a relocation budget.

### 10. Methodology — `/methodology/`

**Search/user job:** Verify how the numbers were produced.

Required sections: purpose, basket definitions, household archetypes, source hierarchy, geography, collection, normalization, currency conversion, tax treatment, aggregation, outlier handling, modeling, confidence formula, update schedule, known limitations and change log.

### 11. Sources — `/sources/`

A human-readable data catalog listing each source, licence/use rights, geographic level, covered variables, update frequency, last successful ingestion and where it appears. A source logo wall is not a substitute for this catalog.

### 12. About and editorial policy — `/about/`

State who runs the site, who reviews methodology/content, how corrections work, how commercial relationships are handled and how to contact the team. Trust-sensitive financial decisions need accountable ownership.

## Content system

### Reusable data-led blocks

- Direct-answer summary.
- Scenario/assumption chip row.
- Category contribution chart.
- Monthly budget table.
- Item-price table.
- Salary-equivalence bridge.
- Evidence and confidence panel.
- Source footnotes.
- Related entity/comparison links.

These blocks should be consistent across pages, but each template must arrange them around a different user job. Reusing the same hero and card grid on every route makes the product feel unchanged and weakens search-intent clarity.

### Editorial blocks

- What drives the difference.
- What could change your result.
- Geographic caveats.
- Data gaps.
- Calculation example.
- Reviewed-by and change history.

Editorial text should be produced from the verified data and then human-reviewed. It must not claim local knowledge the dataset does not contain.

## Data requirements before public scale

### Minimum record model

Each price or index observation needs:

- Location entity and geographic level: city proper, metro, region or country.
- Category, item, unit and quantity.
- Native value and currency.
- Observation period, ingestion date and source.
- Collection method: official, licensed provider, retailer scrape/API, partner, user submission or model.
- Sample/observation count where available.
- Low/median/high or dispersion where available.
- Quality flags and confidence components.
- Licence and permitted display/redistribution status.

### Source hierarchy

1. Official statistical agencies and administrative datasets.
2. Clearly licensed commercial/provider data.
3. Reputable market and retailer sources with lawful use.
4. Moderated first-party submissions.
5. Modeled estimates, used only when labeled and never to imply direct observation.

No production source should be selected only because it is easy to scrape. Rights, stability, geographic fit and update cadence are product requirements.

### Confidence model

The public confidence explanation should combine:

- Source reliability.
- Recency.
- Number of independent observations.
- Geographic match.
- Agreement/dispersion.
- Percentage of the displayed basket directly covered.

The overall comparison confidence cannot be higher than its weakest material category without an explanation.

## Calculation principles

1. Preserve category weights for the selected household and lifestyle rather than multiplying salary by one universal index.
2. Show housing both inside and outside the comparison because it often dominates the result.
3. Treat childcare and private education as conditional high-impact categories.
4. Keep taxes outside the cost basket and add them in the take-home calculation.
5. Keep market salary outside salary equivalence; show it as separate context if a valid source exists.
6. Apply currency conversion after storing native values, with a dated FX rate.
7. Round consumer results honestly; apparent precision must not exceed data quality.
8. Provide a range when sampling uncertainty or scenario sensitivity is material.

## SEO strategy

### Search-intent map

| Intent | Target page | Example query |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Broad task | Homepage / compare hub | living cost comparison, compare cost of living |
| Named pair | Pair page | London vs Amsterdam cost of living |
| One city | City profile | cost of living in Amsterdam |
| Salary decision | Salary calculator or pair section | equivalent salary London to Amsterdam |
| Index/research | City index | cost of living index by city |
| Discovery | Ranking | most affordable cities in Europe |
| Explanation | Guide/methodology | how is cost of living calculated |

The common English phrasing is **"cost of living comparison."** The domain can remain `livingcostcomparison.com`, but page titles, headings and natural copy should use both forms without forcing the domain wording into every sentence.

### Indexation gate

An entity or pair page is indexable only if all are true:

1. Core categories meet the coverage threshold.
2. The page has a current update date and evidence panel.
3. It produces at least one original, defensible computed insight.
4. The result is useful without login or payment.
5. The canonical is unique and present in the correct sitemap.
6. Links connect it to city/country parents and relevant comparisons.
7. There are no unresolved critical data warnings.

Prototype data, empty states, search/filter permutations and reversed duplicates must not be indexed.

### Internal-link system

- City profile → top comparisons from that city, country hub and relevant guide.
- Pair page → both city profiles, two country hubs and nearby alternative cities.
- Country hub → covered cities, within-country comparisons and regional rankings.
- Guide → calculator state or examples that let the reader apply the lesson.
- Index → city profiles and a two-city compare action.

Links must be normal crawlable anchors and contextually named. Do not rely only on JavaScript state or a site search box.

### Technical rules

- Server-render the direct answer and meaningful supporting content.
- Use one deterministic canonical per city pair.
- Keep query-string scenarios out of the index and canonical them to the base pair page.
- Split sitemaps by template when the inventory grows; include only canonical, indexable URLs.
- Use Breadcrumb structured data on entity pages.
- Use Dataset structured data only where a real downloadable or described dataset exists—not to decorate prototype values.
- Structured data must match visible content and must not contain self-awarded ratings.
- Titles, H1s and descriptions should be template-consistent but factually unique.
- Preserve fast Core Web Vitals by avoiding a heavy map or chart library above the fold.

### Helpful-content standard

Google's published guidance favors original, useful, people-first content and warns against producing large amounts of search-first automation. For this project, that means page count follows data quality—not the reverse. Each generated page needs a clear audience, a complete answer, transparent creation/review and evidence a competitor summary cannot provide.

## What to emulate and what to avoid

### Emulate

- Numbeo's data depth and quality metadata.
- Expatistan's disclosure of underlying prices and surveys.
- Livingcost.org's city/country entity architecture.
- NerdWallet's fast direct answer and tangible item table.
- SmartAsset's household inputs and tax awareness.
- Bankrate's editorial accountability.
- CityCostComparison's shareable no-signup flow.
- MIT's household archetypes and technical documentation.
- Mercer's distinction between shopper types and major relocation expenses.

### Avoid

- Dense navigation and dashboards before the user sees an answer.
- Thousands of pages built from the same generic paragraphs.
- Mixing taxes, quality of life and market salary into one unexplained score.
- Exact-looking numbers without dates, geography or uncertainty.
- Calling modeled data "local prices."
- Reverse pair URLs competing in search.
- Lead-generation interruptions before the core answer.
- Rankings with subjective titles and hidden weighting.
- Adding tools only because competitors have them.

## Recommended release plan

### Phase 0 — Make the current prototype safe and coherent

- Keep the current illustrative dataset explicitly labeled and out of search indexing.
- Fix reverse-pair canonical behavior.
- Ensure every navigable pair is either in the sitemap or intentionally non-indexable.
- Remove Dataset claims until production data exists.
- Replace hard-coded years/dates with data-driven timestamps.
- Keep local and reference currencies distinct.
- Finish the different page roles already established in the interface.

**Exit condition:** The prototype accurately describes itself and cannot create misleading indexed pages.

### Phase 1 — Production data beta: 15–25 cities

- Select sources and document licences.
- Implement the observation, source and confidence model.
- Cover at least seven major categories with item detail.
- Launch household/lifestyle scenarios and shareable results.
- Publish complete methodology, sources and correction policy.
- Hand-review every city and pair chosen for indexation.

Choose cities from a focused relocation corridor rather than a random global list—for example UK/Western Europe plus a small set of major remote-work and migration destinations.

**Exit condition:** A user can verify where every material number came from and obtain a meaningfully personalized result.

### Phase 2 — Organic growth: 50–100 cities

- Expand from Search Console demand and data confidence.
- Add country hubs and curated high-demand pair pages.
- Publish the first original-data guides.

## Major risks and mitigations

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data licensing or redistribution limits | Can invalidate the product after launch | Record licence and permitted use per source before ingestion |
| False precision | Damages trust and user decisions | Show ranges, dates, sample sizes and confidence; round appropriately |
| Geography mismatch | City, metro and region prices can differ sharply | Display the geographic unit next to every result and avoid silent fallback |
| Programmatic thin content | Creates poor UX and search-quality risk | Use the indexation gate and human review for early inventory |
| Currency volatility | Makes cross-country results change for reasons unrelated to costs | Date FX separately and allow native-currency inspection |
| Tax complexity | A simple formula can be materially wrong | Limit tax support by jurisdiction and show assumptions/status |
| Over-expansion | Many half-working tools dilute the brand | Require a distinct user job, dataset and success metric for each tool |
| Monetization bias | Lead-gen can undermine trust | Keep the complete core answer accessible; disclose commercial relationships |

## Immediate decisions required from the owner

These choices determine the production data and launch corridor:

1. **First market corridor:** global from day one, or a narrower set such as UK/Europe, US domestic, or South Asia-to-Gulf/Europe relocation.
2. **Data budget:** official/open sources only, licensed provider budget, or a hybrid.
3. **Tax scope:** living-cost comparison first, or take-home calculations in the first public beta.
4. **Review capacity:** who owns source review, city-page approval and corrections.

The recommended default is a hybrid source strategy, 15–25 high-confidence international cities, living-cost equivalence at launch and take-home calculations only for jurisdictions whose tax rules can be maintained reliably.
