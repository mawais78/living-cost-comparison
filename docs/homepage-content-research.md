# Homepage content research and implementation brief

Research date: 26 September 2026  
Website: https://livingcostcomparison.com/  
Scope: Homepage copy, search intent, answer clarity, claims and internal links. This brief combines current primary-source competitor research, official search documentation and inspection of the application. It is qualitative research, not a keyword-volume or traffic study.

## Recommendation

Make the homepage answer one practical question: **How would my monthly living costs and required take-home income change if I moved to another city?**

Lead with a working city selector, plain language about the comparison, and the actual coverage. Then explain the eight expense categories, household and lifestyle presets, equivalent-income calculation and omitted costs. Keep the existing visual design while replacing abstract or aspirational claims with statements supported by the current application.

The defensible differentiation is a clear planning workflow: compare two cities, inspect the modeled monthly budget, estimate equivalent take-home income, and understand the assumptions. Database size, live local prices, survey rigor and personalized tax calculations are not established advantages of the current product.

## Audience and qualitative intent map

The priority audience is people considering a move between two known cities, including international movers, workers evaluating an offer and people selecting a remote-work base. Couples and families are relevant secondary audiences, provided the family preset is described as a model and its childcare/education limitations are visible.

These query groups describe observed product and search-result patterns. They do not establish search volume, difficulty, conversion rate or ranking potential.

| Intent | Example language | Homepage answer and destination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compare two locations | cost of living comparison; compare cost of living between cities | A clear H1 and immediate city selector; link to the comparison tool |
| Explore a destination budget | cost of living in a city; monthly living expenses; living costs with rent | Explain rent inclusion and the eight categories; link to city pages and the city index |
| Evaluate an income change | equivalent salary; salary comparison between cities; how much do I need to earn after moving | Explain a take-home-income planning target and link to the salary calculator |
| Match a household | cost of living for one person, a couple or a family of four | State the available presets and their limits, without implying item-by-item household modeling |
| Understand the calculation | what is included; does it include taxes; how accurate is it; why do calculators differ | Short direct answers, visible estimate disclosure and a methodology link |
| Plan the whole move | relocation budget; moving abroad costs; rent deposits | Distinguish recurring living costs from one-time moving costs and link to the existing planning guide |

Use the exact primary phrase naturally in the title, H1 and introduction. Supporting language should come from the user's task: rent, groceries, transport, utilities, monthly budget, household, take-home pay and destination city. Repeating keyword variants through every section would add little information.

## Current competitor observations

All observations below were checked on the competitors' own public pages on the research date. Their self-reported coverage and methodology describe their products; they are not independent quality assessments or evidence for this site's estimates.

| Primary source | Observed approach | Homepage implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Numbeo city comparison](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/comparison.jsp) | Leads with current and comparison cities, followed by everyday expenses and a cost index. The framing centers on moving, budgeting and purchasing power. | Put the city choice first and name concrete costs in the introduction. |
| [Expatistan comparison homepage](https://www.expatistan.com/cost-of-living) | Connects city comparisons to moving and salary conversion. Shows crowdsourced price/contributor counts and examples of recent comparisons. | Connect cost comparison with income planning, but do not borrow crowdsourcing or popularity claims without this site's own evidence. |
| [Livingcost calculator](https://livingcost.org/cost) | Offers global city/country coverage, an after-tax monthly salary input, a defined consumer basket and directories. | Global coverage and net-income language fit the task; this site should state its own coverage rather than compete through unsupported scale claims. |
| [Livingcost methodology](https://livingcost.org/about) | Describes crowdsourced/public data, a prediction process, basket coefficients and limitations related to lifestyle and geography. | The model and its limitations should be understandable before readers rely on the numbers. Different baskets explain why tools can disagree. |
| [NerdWallet calculator](https://www.nerdwallet.com/cost-of-living-calculator) | Uses current city, destination and pre-tax income. Shows an equivalent-income result, expense tables, named C2ER sourcing, a metro-area housing caveat and explicit tax exclusion. | A headline answer is more useful when category detail and inclusion/exclusion notes sit nearby. This site's take-home calculation must be distinguished from gross salary. |
| [SmartAsset calculator](https://smartasset.com/mortgage/cost-of-living-calculator) | Asks for income, adults and children; includes tax, housing and food calculations with assumptions. | Household composition and tax treatment are important user questions. This site's preset-only model must not imply SmartAsset-style tax calculations. |

Across these products, tool use is the primary intent. Supporting content earns its place when it helps a reader interpret a result or decide what to verify. The homepage does not need to reproduce an entire cost-of-living textbook.

## Product evidence and claim boundaries

The application audit at this date found:

- 250 city records across 82 countries and territories. Use dynamic counts in the page rather than hardcoded marketing totals.
- Eight modeled categories: housing, groceries, dining out, transport, utilities and connectivity, healthcare, personal care, and leisure.
- Values stored as USD-equivalent planning estimates for the September 2026 edition. City currency labels do not constitute a live currency-conversion feature or dated exchange-rate evidence.
- Household presets for one person, a couple and a family of four. The model scales the base basket with factors of 1, 1.55 and 2.2.
- Lean, balanced and comfortable lifestyle presets with factors of 0.82, 1 and 1.25.
- A salary tool that multiplies current monthly take-home income by the destination/current modeled-budget ratio and rounds the result. Annual take-home is the monthly result multiplied by 12.
- No separate tax engine, childcare or tuition category, item-level observation citations, contributor/sample counts, live local quotes, or native-price/FX audit trail.
- Dining, healthcare, personal care and leisure are allocated from a flexible-spending amount. Those category values must not be described as separately surveyed or verified local prices.

The household and lifestyle factors are applied across the basket. They change modeled spending totals, but they are not a granular family budget and largely preserve relative city differences, apart from rounding. Avoid suggesting that the family setting independently models local childcare, school fees or neighborhood choices.

Safe language includes **modeled monthly estimate**, **planning baseline**, **USD-equivalent**, **household preset**, **equivalent take-home income** and **compare the same assumptions**. Avoid **real-time**, **verified prices**, **most accurate**, **observed local prices**, **live exchange rates**, **complete family budget**, **tax-adjusted salary** and **popular comparisons** without supporting evidence. Use **featured comparisons** for editorially selected pairs.

## Recommended content structure

1. **Hero and metadata:** Name the cost-of-living comparison task directly. Explain that users can compare modeled monthly expenses and estimate equivalent take-home pay. Retain the city selector beside the text.
2. **Immediate explanation:** Answer what the tool compares, which household presets exist, and what the income result means. Link to the city comparison, salary tool and methodology.
3. **Expense coverage:** Name all eight actual categories. Explain housing and commute choices without claiming users can edit individual assumptions when the interface only offers presets.
4. **City exploration:** Retain the chart and city links. Label amounts as monthly USD-equivalent model estimates and state the one-person, balanced scenario.
5. **Featured pairs:** Keep useful city-pair links with descriptive labels. Avoid inferring usage or demand from an editorial selection.
6. **How to compare:** Give an actionable sequence: select cities, match presets, inspect expense differences, estimate take-home income, and replace major assumptions with current local quotes.
7. **Worked salary example:** Show the ratio calculation and identify it as illustrative. Explain that it estimates proportional spending power, not employer pay or the gross salary needed after local taxes.
8. **Whole-move checklist:** Separate monthly costs, one-time move/setup costs and after-tax resources. Explicitly identify childcare, tuition, taxes, benefits and moving costs as items to assess separately.
9. **Trust and FAQs:** State the model, currency and edition, and link to methodology. Use direct questions and self-contained answers about inclusions, rent, presets, currency, salary, accuracy and inflation.
10. **Final action:** Invite a city comparison using the same household and lifestyle assumptions.

The implementation should preserve useful internal links to `/compare-cities`, `/salary-comparison`, `/cost-of-living-index`, `/methodology`, the existing guides and relevant city-pair pages. The home page introduces these tasks; their dedicated pages carry the detailed workflows.

## SEO, answer engines and AI search

The official guidance below was independently verified in the parent research for this implementation:

- [Google's AI optimization guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says established SEO practices still apply, with useful original content and no special AI markup or files required. Plain answers, accurate terminology and accessible supporting detail are appropriate improvements; they do not guarantee AI citations.
- [Google's helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) supports useful, trustworthy content that demonstrates its basis. For this homepage, that means describing the actual tool and disclosing modeled estimates instead of presenting planned capabilities as current facts.
- [Google Search documentation updates](https://developers.google.com/search/updates) record the discontinuation of FAQ rich results on 7 May 2026 and removal of the related documentation on 15 June 2026. Keep FAQs for readers and answer clarity. Do not promise an FAQ rich-result benefit.

Use a coherent WebSite, Organization, WebPage and ItemList structured-data graph that matches visible content. Do not invent review scores, author credentials, dataset provenance or claimed data quality. Structured data helps describe the page; it does not make its estimates verified or guarantee enhanced search presentation.

## Supporting cost-of-living context

- [BLS consumer-spending analysis](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/housing-and-transportation-accounted-for-50-percent-of-household-spending-in-2024.htm) supports the importance of housing and transport in **U.S. household spending in 2024**. It must not be presented as a universal allocation for every country or as a source for the site's city estimates.
- [BLS CPI questions and answers](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/questions-and-answers.htm) support explaining that price change over time and comparisons between places answer different questions. Avoid describing this city's cross-sectional model as an inflation tracker.

These are contextual references, not provenance for the application's modeled amounts. The homepage should make that distinction whenever it links to external evidence.

## Measurement after implementation

Establish a baseline and monitor Search Console impressions, clicks and query families for the homepage; city-selector starts and comparison completions; clicks into salary and methodology; and the share of users continuing to city/pair pages. Assess whether more specific copy improves task completion before adding more text.

This research does not establish keyword volumes, competitive ranking difficulty, traffic forecasts, measured popularity of selected city pairs or a guaranteed ranking/AI-visibility improvement. Those claims require separate data. The remaining substantive trust limitation is the absence of documented city-level price provenance; clearer copy makes the current model honest and easier to use but does not resolve that data gap.

## Implemented copy and verification

- Title: **Cost of Living Comparison Calculator | Compare Cities**.
- H1: **Compare the cost of living between cities.**
- Meta description: **Compare the cost of living in 250 cities. Explore monthly budgets including rent, groceries and transport, and estimate equivalent take-home pay for a move.** The city count is generated from the dataset; the current description is 156 characters.
- Rewrote the homepage sections, comparison-launcher labels and eight FAQs. Added the salary example from the same city records and rounding convention as the calculator: $6,500 × $3,420 ÷ $4,000 ≈ $5,560 net per month.
- Coverage counts, model edition, category labels and example amounts are derived from the existing application data. Retained the established design and added responsive styling for the worked example.
- The homepage selector uses neutral first/second-city labels because pair pages normalize their URL order. Instructions explain setting the current city and destination with the calculator's swap control.

Validation completed on 26 September 2026: ESLint passed; the final production build passed type checking and generated 385 static pages. Production HTML checks confirmed one H1, the homepage canonical, consistent description metadata, eight server-rendered FAQ answers, parseable structured data and 26 working internal destinations. Browser checks at desktop and mobile widths confirmed no horizontal page overflow, a readable salary example, an expanding FAQ answer and successful city-selector navigation to the chosen comparison.
